# The Cash-Only Ford Loyalist

## At a glance
A woman in her 50s from southeast Arkansas who drove trucks for most of her working life, then did store-to-store retail merchandising. Chronic pain and a run of health problems now keep her at home doing piecemeal online work. She is getting by on very little, with SNAP and Medicaid filling the gap. She buys what is cheap, proven and comfortable, sticks with brands that have worked for her (Ford, Colgate), and judges almost every purchase by whether it does the job without costing more than it has to.

## Behavioral (primary)
- **What she's trying to get done (JTBD):** keep the bills paid and the household stocked until retirement, which she frames as a countdown. Success means rent covered, basics in the pantry and bathroom, a truck that runs, and less pain.
- **What she uses / has tried:**
  - *Vehicles:* a used 1998 Ford Ranger with a manual gearbox and no radio, taken from a family member after her previous Chevrolet truck died of a gas-line fault and left her without wheels for about three months. She has owned several Fords, including Mustangs, and dislikes Chevrolet and GMC. Her next vehicle will be a used, mid-range Ford, and it will be an automatic because of her back.
  - *Shopping channels:* Amazon, Walmart, Dollar Tree, thrift and Goodwill stores, and the flea market on weekends. She shops online for most things.
  - *Personal care:* dollar-store soaps, Alberto VO5 or Suave shampoo, or whatever off-brand bottle Dollar Tree has. She settled on Colgate after deciding it works better than Aquafresh. Plus Covergirl foundation, L.A. Colors eyeliner, an inexpensive face moisturizer, a L'Oréal hair mist and plenty of body lotion. First-aid basics are always on hand: rubbing alcohol, peroxide, Band-Aids, pain patches.
  - *Household:* Palmolive and Dawn dish soap (Dawn also goes into a homemade bug spray), Swiffer, bleach and liquid detergent. The pantry always has flour, sugar, cornmeal, vegetable and olive oil, sandwich bread, ranch dressing, sweet-and-sour sauce and mustard.
  - *Media:* Yahoo News and Yahoo Mail, Gmail, Facebook and Instagram, and live TV over the internet with no antenna. Streams on Netflix, Paramount+, Hulu, Peacock and sometimes Disney. Her shows are reality competitions (Survivor, The Amazing Race, anything with Gordon Ramsay), late-night talk shows, PBS, cooking shows and historical dramas such as Downton Abbey. She checks Ancestry.com for new hints.
- **How she decides:** on her own. Price and budget come first, then proven performance and comfort or ease of upkeep. She tries a product, compares it with what she had, and stays put once something works. She pays cash and avoids taking on debt.
- **Frequency / cadence:** daily grocery and cooking decisions, a regular restock of household basics, occasional thrift and flea-market trips, and rare big purchases. Vehicles are replaced only when one breaks.

## Contextual (primary)
- **Trigger:** necessity. Something breaks or runs out, or her health forces a change, such as needing an automatic transmission or giving up heels for tennis shoes.
- **Constraints:**
  - *Money:* she reports needing at least about $1,200 a month and earning less than that. Last year's income was around $12k.
  - *Housing:* rent is $550 a month, and she has thought about moving into public housing.
  - *Debt:* a small credit-card balance and roughly $600 owed to the IRS, being paid back monthly.
  - *Health:* chronic pain, back trouble and a hernia limit what she can wear and how much she can move or travel.
  - *Distance:* she lives about two hours from her children.
- **Environment:** at home on a laptop between bouts of online work, Facebook and sitting on the porch. Purchases are mostly online or at discount chains, and she researches very little beyond her own past experience.

## Demographics

- **Family:** divorced twice and single for five years. Two adult children and four grandchildren. Her elderly adoptive father lives nearby.
- **Location and work:** grew up in southeast Arkansas with stretches out West and recently moved to a new city and state. Was a truck driver, then a retail merchandiser, and now works from home online.

## What makes them bounce
- New-vehicle pricing and insurance costs. She cannot see herself buying new.
- Anything sold on credit or financing, given her preference for cash.
- Chevrolet and GMC.
- Products that are uncomfortable or hard on a bad back: heels, manual transmissions, anything physically demanding.
- Fashion-led or status-led pitches. She cares about looking decent, not about trends.
- Anything that assumes travel, a vacation budget or an active lifestyle. She hasn't had a real vacation in 15–20 years.

## What would actually convince them
- A low price at places she already shops: Walmart, Amazon, Dollar Tree.
- Evidence that it outperforms the cheaper or competing option, the way Colgate beat Aquafresh for her.
- Durability and easy maintenance. Her reason for liking Fords is that they are easy to keep running.
- Comfort that suits pain and limited mobility.
- Practical, no-nonsense claims that it will get the job done.

## Negative experiences and constraints (required)
- **Current frustration:** she can't cover her bills on what she earns, and inflation makes it worse. Online work is often unavailable when she checks.
- **Recent setback:** her Chevy truck failed, leaving her without a vehicle for about three months. She also ended up owing the IRS about $600 on a roughly $12k income, which she feels is unfair.
- **Persistent constraint:** six years of health problems (chronic pain, back, hernia) limit work, clothing, driving and travel. She lives two hours from her children and grandchildren.
- **Pattern that doesn't work:** she has lived through a long history of domestic violence in relationships, and leaving when things went bad became her pattern. She is now single by choice and wary of new partners. She knows a cheap old manual truck is wrong for her back but is making do until she can afford something else.
