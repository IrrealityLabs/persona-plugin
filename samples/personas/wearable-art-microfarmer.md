# The Wearable-Art Microfarmer

## At a glance
Lena is a 48-year-old self-employed owner of a small hospitality business in the US. She runs it from a home office while building a microfarm (goats and chickens now, bees and Highland cows planned) and looking after several disabled animals. She is a self-described jack-of-all-trades who goes deep on whatever has her attention. The trait that most shapes how she reacts in a category: she treats a purchase, especially clothing, as self-expression and art, and she wants freedom of expression without being stared at for it.

## Behavioral (primary)
- **What they're trying to get done (JTBD):** Keep a home business and a growing farm running on one tight daily loop, and keep enough calm, creative and outdoor time to feel good. Financially, she wants all debt (student loans, two car loans, other debt) cleared within about five years so she can relocate. She measures success by being debt-free in time and having a calm, safe, healthy household. More money would be welcome, but she says life comes before finances.
- **What they use / have tried:**
  - **Skin care:** a 15-minute routine every day and a roughly 2-hour deep routine once a week. Current products she named: Truly after-shave oil, Clinique toner, a Ren scrubbing cleanser, an Ahava moisturizing cleanser, and a Dr. B Lab (as transcribed) facial cleanser. She switches between many brands.
  - **Apparel:** she owns expensive shoes, purses and other pieces but says they don't make her feel better. She has no go-to store. She shops by occasion, going to whichever shop she knows has a wide selection of what she needs (for example, cocktail dresses). She needs form-fitting cuts for a fuller figure with a small waist.
  - **Food:** she is vegetarian and buys fruit and vegetables weekly, plus cheeses and charcuterie-board items. She leans toward pastries and patisserie. She tries new products often and recently found an Indian-spiced popcorn she now loves.
  - **Media:** she mostly does self-directed research. Her current obsession is mountaineering and alpinism: Everest, Sherpas, gear, acclimatizing. TikTok is the one app she scrolls aimlessly, though she says she uses social media only lightly.
  - **Career/education:** she has studied nursing and pre-med, holds a surgical tech certification (and has scrubbed into the OR), has a psychology degree, and now runs a hospitality business.
- **How they decide:**
  - **Big purchases:** she decides alone and arrives knowing what she wants. When her old car needed a major repair, she worked out that buying new made more sense. She already knew the make, model and style, browsed local dealerships online, and held to a list of non-negotiable features.
  - **Discretionary purchases:** she follows curiosity and how a product feels to her, not the brand or the price tag.
  - **In general:** she says she puts a lot of thought into most decisions but sometimes lets herself be impulsive.
  - **Civic choices:** she can stay undecided until the last minute. She did not vote in the last election after going back and forth up to that morning.
- **Frequency / cadence:** Animal care several times a day. Bookkeeping, inventory, orders and marketing in the home office in blocks between animal care. Errands capped at about 2–3 hours a day. A hard stop on physical tasks around 7–8 pm. Groceries weekly, skin care daily plus weekly. She sees an out-of-state friend roughly every month or two and does frequent group outings with family.

## Contextual (primary)
- **Trigger:** Something practical breaking (the car repair pushed a car purchase). An occasion that needs a specific outfit. A new rabbit hole or creative project. Farm expansion (bees, cows). The relocation goal shapes her finances.
- **Constraints:**
  - **Time:** her day is split between animals and business, with no slack.
  - **Money:** she is servicing debt and two car loans against a five-year payoff goal. She calls herself comfortable but not flush.
  - **Fit:** her body shape limits which clothes work.
  - **Attention and sensory load:** she is neurodivergent and sensitive, and wants calm, safe, non-chaotic environments.
- **Environment:** She researches at home on the computer in evening wind-down hours or wherever a rabbit hole takes her. She browses dealerships online before visiting. She shops apparel in person while out running errands. Her ideal setting is calm, unhurried and outdoors.

## What makes them bounce
- Fashion or products marketed by gender, age or race, or sold as "for" a narrow type of person.
- Status pricing whose only claim is prestige. She owns luxury pieces and says they don't make her feel any better.
- Clothing that isn't cut for a curvy figure with a small waist and full hips.
- Chaotic, crowded or unsafe environments and experiences (her ideal city is calm and safe).
- "Lesser of two evils" framing. She will walk away rather than pick the least-bad option.
- Anything that adds new debt or works against her five-year payoff-and-relocate plan.

## What would actually convince them
- Products that let her express herself creatively and fit well: form-fitting, inclusive sizing and broad selection in one place.
- Depth she can research. She enjoys learning everything about a topic, so detailed, knowledgeable content (gear, process, provenance) suits her.
- Something new and sensorially interesting to try (like the spiced popcorn), especially vegetarian food, cheese, pastry.
- Experiences that are outdoor, serene, cultural and colourful (nature, museums, a calm city, Lisbon).
- Practical value when something breaks. She'll do the cost comparison and go with whatever makes sense, as long as her non-negotiables are met.
- Brands that signal fairness and equity in more than slogans.
