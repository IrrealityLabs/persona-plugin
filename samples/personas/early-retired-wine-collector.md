# The Frugal Cellar-Keeper

## At a glance
Graham (pseudonym) took early retirement about a year and a half ago in Wellington, New Zealand, and is bridging to the state pension, due next year. He fills his days with housework, gardening, library non-fiction, selling his late parents' belongings online and a hobby-scale business importing champagne stoppers. His defining trait is a practical, value-per-year frugality that sits alongside one deliberate indulgence: a wine cellar built over roughly four decades that he rations rather than drinks.

## Behavioral (primary)
- **What they're trying to get done (JTBD):** Live comfortably on a fixed, modest budget (he puts core spending at roughly a couple of thousand NZD a month, before health or one-off costs) while staying in full control of his own time. Success = no debt, a cash buffer, no health scares, and the occasional good bottle shared with friends.
- **What they use / have tried:**
  - *Money:* no loans; credit cards paid in full monthly, used for nearly everything to collect rewards and keep cash in the bank longer. Investments split roughly 30% cash / 70% managed funds, plus KiwiSaver; part of the cash held in a breakable term deposit as an emergency fund. Tracks it all in a spreadsheet and checks investment apps often; calls himself a semi-active trader.
  - *Shopping:* weekly or fortnightly grocery delivery; buys staples, picks up items like blueberries or smoked salmon only when they are on special. Clothing bought online after searching for deals, mostly sale-season Puma track pants and T-shirts, Levi's jeans; wears garments until they are worn out (a home shirt about 30 years old).
  - *Personal care:* minimal. Dove soap chosen partly because it leaves less soap scum to clean; a long-lasting deodorant chosen for value; sunscreen and a cap only as needed.
  - *Cars:* holds cars for a very long time to minimise cost per year: a second-hand classic sports coupe, then its turbo version for about 18 years, then a compact German performance hatch he has had about 14 years. Has done car-club track and ice driving; wants handling and performance in a practical, not-too-big, not-too-costly-to-run package.
  - *Information:* Google and Bing for DIY fixes (says he can't really afford tradespeople) and plant pests or disease; library books weekly; lots of time on Trade Me listing items and answering buyer questions.
  - *Media:* mostly non-fiction (science, history, wine); occasional comic novel. Dislikes most movies but enjoys concert films (named a remastered Talking Heads film). Light BBC comedy on TV as background while doing email.
- **How they decide:** Solo, analytically, with an explicit long-run cost/benefit frame (cost per year of ownership, durability, value per use). Describes himself as a critical thinker who challenges ideas; discovering this at university was a life milestone for him. of about 30 years, Alice (pseudonym).
- **Frequency / cadence:** Daily home routine (tea in bed reading, housework, short daily gardening, bread maker every 2–3 days; he does all the cooking and plans for leftovers). Weekly library trip. Annual long weekend at a wine-region town within driving distance of Wellington with friends Neil and Megan (about 10–12 years running). Trips to Hawke's Bay roughly every six months to see Alice's family and his friend Glenn.

## Contextual (primary)
- **Trigger:** Recent early retirement (his job no longer exists; whether this was voluntary is unclear) plus the death of both parents (the last about a year ago), which has left a house of belongings to clear and sell. Spring-cleaning and recycling, including stripping old electronics for circuit-board recycling.
- **Constraints:** Fixed income until the pension arrives next year; watches money closely and buys few luxuries. Cannot justify buying premium wine now, so the cellar has to last. A Champagne trip is a dream that requires saving. Some health concern (wants check-ups to come back clean).
- **Environment:** At home in Wellington, researching on a computer via search engines, buying online for delivery, often multitasking with TV on. Social life is wine, cooking and long talks at friends' homes.

## Demographics
Mid-60s (will be almost 70 in five years), male, Wellington, New Zealand. Biology and geology double major who never worked in science; about 10 years in radio (started via student radio), took redundancy, then marketing and communications with a science angle (food safety, a telecommunications company, government and large national/international employers), plus about 10 years self-employed. Lives with his long-term unmarried partner, who still works. Grew up in a lower-income family; parents split when he was young and as the eldest he helped look after his younger siblings.

## What makes them bounce
- Bureaucracy and big-organisation process, in government or private companies; he explicitly does not miss it.
- Anything he reads as a handout rather than a hand-up, or spending without a clear return (financial or social).
- Emotional or sentimental appeals; he distrusts what he sees as unearned emotion.
- Fashion, image or status pitches; skincare and grooming upsells.
- Fiction-style storytelling as a substitute for facts.
- Premium prices without a durability or cost-per-use case; anything that adds running costs.

## What would actually convince them
- A clear, checkable value argument: lasts longer, cheaper per year, less effort (as with his soap and deodorant choices).
- Deals and sales he finds himself through search; half-price-on-sale events.
- Factual, non-fiction framing with history or science behind it, especially around wine and Champagne.
- Practical performance: things that work well without luxury extras.
- Experiences that fit his existing social rituals (wine towns, sharing bottles with old friends).

## Negative experiences and constraints (required)
- **Current frustration:** The mess of clearing out his late parents' house and their belongings; it hangs over him even in retirement. Annoyed with the state of New Zealand's economy and government spending.
- **Recent setback:** Loss of his job leading into early retirement, and the death of both parents, the last about a year ago.
- **Persistent constraint:** Living on savings until the pension starts next year; cannot afford the quality of wine he would like, or tradespeople for repairs; the Champagne trip needs saving for.
- **Pattern they keep repeating that doesn't work:** Rationing the good bottles in his cellar so carefully that some may pass their best while he waits (he notes some are ready now and he cannot drink them all).
- **Background hardship:** A lower-income childhood with a father who drank and was repeatedly fired, and parents who split early, leaving him in a caretaking role for his siblings.
